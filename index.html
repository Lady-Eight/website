MSc research · Digital twin · Machine learning

A digital twin for every lost litre

Non-revenue water is water a utility pays to treat and pump, then never bills: lost to leaks, illegal connections and tired meters. In many Kenyan utilities it is more than a third of everything produced.

My MSc builds a hydraulic digital twin for Gatundu Water & Sanitation Company (GATWASCO) in Kiambu County: a calibrated, data-fed copy of the real network that shows where water goes missing and lets engineers test fixes before digging a single trench. The next layer is machine learning, which turns the twin from a mirror into an early warning system.

1

The physical network

Pipes, valves, tanks, meters and the people they serve.

2

Sensing & field data

Flow and pressure readings, customer and asset surveys, GIS mapping.

3

Hydraulic model

A calibrated model that predicts how the network should behave, hour by hour.

4

Machine learning

Compares reality with the twin, learns normal, and flags the abnormal.

5

Decisions

Where to send crews, where to zone, which meters to replace first.

Good questions

Before you book

The things people usually ask on the first call.

Do you work outside Kenya?
Yes. I have field experience in Kenya, the DR Congo and Tanzania, delegate experience in Brazil, and I take on-site deployments and remote advisory anywhere.
What can you supervise or inspect?
Markets, agro-processing plants, haysheds, livestock yards, roads and water infrastructure: daily supervision, quality checks, progress verification for funders and clear inspection reports.
What does a digital twin need to start?
Less than most utilities fear: a network map, some flow and pressure readings, billing data and a willing team. We start with one zone, prove the value, then scale.
Do you work with NGOs and donor projects?
Often. I have worked across government, private contractors, community organisations and UN agencies, so I know how to report to a ministry and a donor in the same week.
How fast do you reply?
Usually within 48 hours. If I am on a remote site, WhatsApp is the fastest way to reach me.
